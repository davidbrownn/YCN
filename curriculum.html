Open curriculum

Every lesson we teach, free to take.

17 lessons across four tracks, built for grades 3–5 and written to the same shape: a five-minute warm-up, ten minutes of instruction, two games, and a wrap-up. You do not need to be a YCN chapter to use any of it.

  • Local government 6
  • State government 3
  • Federal government 5
  • Elections and voting 3

Local government

The decisions closest to a student — who fixes the playground, who runs the library, who sets the rules at their own school.

  • Local government

    City Councils

    Who sits on a city council and how they decide what a city builds. Students pool a budget they cannot stretch, then argue a real problem in front of a student council.

    Grades 3–5~35 minThe Great Budget Debate · Council Pass

    Read the plan→

  • Local government

    The Mayor's Office

    What a mayor actually does, and why two cities the same size can be run completely differently. Compares the strong-mayor and city-manager systems.

    Grades 3–5~35 minCity problem cards · Design your own city

    Read the plan→

  • Local government

    School Boards

    The board that sets the rules of a student's own school — what it controls, who serves on it, and why its members aren't teachers.

    Grades 3–5~35 minWrite a policy · Rapid-fire review

    Read the plan→

  • Local government

    Public Libraries and Parks

    The two public spaces students already use, and the departments that keep them open. Sorts fact from fiction about what a library is allowed to do.

    Grades 3–5~35 minFiction or Non-Fiction · Design a park

    Read the plan→

  • Local government

    Public Transit Authorities

    The agencies that run buses and trains, and who depends on them. Students act out the same trip twice — once with an agency, once without.

    Grades 3–5~35 minDesign a transit agency · With and without

    Read the plan→

  • Local government

    Police and Firefighters

    What first responders do and how the two roles differ. Students sort real scenarios into police, firefighter, or both — and find out how often the answer is both.

    Grades 3–5~35 minDraw a first responder · Scenario sort

    Read the plan→

State government

The layer most adults can't name either — legislatures, courts, attorneys general, and the agencies that issue a driver's licence.

  • State government

    State Legislatures

    Students research a real committee in their own state legislature, find a bill it is considering, and argue for or against it in front of the class.

    Grades 3–5~40 minCommittee research · Propose and vote

    Read the plan→

  • State government

    State Courts and the Attorney General

    Who enforces a state's laws, and who decides what they mean. Opens with a genuinely hard question: would you prosecute your own friend?

    Grades 3–5~35 minSAG Says · Courtroom roles

    Read the plan→

  • State government

    State Agencies

    The DMV, the health department, the transportation department — the bodies that carry out state law and touch daily life more often than the legislature does.

    Grades 3–5~35 minMatch the agency · Run your own

    Read the plan→

Federal government

Congress, the committees where bills actually get written, and the departments that carry the results out.

  • Federal government

    Congress: How a Bill Becomes Law

    Our fullest unit. Students draw their own districts, put bills in a hat, and run a mock Congress through the House, the Senate, and a presidential signature — then do it again at state level.

    Grades 3–5Multi-week seriesDraw your district · Mock Congress · State budget

    Read the plan→

  • Federal government

    Congressional Committees

    Why Congress splits itself into small groups before it votes. Students match real bills to the committee that would study them, then sit as the committee themselves.

    Grades 3–5~35 minBill-to-committee match · Committee vote

    Read the plan→

  • Federal government

    Cabinet Departments

    The fifteen departments that advise the president and run the executive branch, covering the five students are most likely to hear about.

    Grades 3–5~35 minDepartment Freeze · Advise the president

    Read the plan→

  • Federal government

    The Department of Education

    What the federal education department does — and, just as usefully, what it doesn't. Students sort decisions between Washington, the district, and their own principal.

    Grades 3–5~35 minWho decides? · Principal for a day

    Read the plan→

  • Federal government

    Health and Human Services

    The department behind disease tracking, food and medicine safety, and medical research, introduced through the sub-agencies students have already heard of.

    Grades 3–5~35 minAgency match · Outbreak response

    Read the plan→

Elections and voting

How a vote turns into an outcome — including the parts of the process that surprise adults.

  • Elections and voting

    The Electoral College

    Why the person with the most votes doesn't automatically win. Walks through electors, the 270 threshold, and what happens in a 269–269 tie.

    Grades 3–5~35 minMock electoral map · Tiebreaker

    Read the plan→

  • Elections and voting

    Ballot Measures and Referendums

    Direct democracy: the questions voters decide themselves, without going through a legislature. Opens with a class vote on who really makes the decisions.

    Grades 3–5~35 minWrite a ballot measure · Class referendum

    Read the plan→

  • Elections and voting

    Campaign Finance

    Where campaign money comes from, what it buys, and the rules around it — starting from the donation ads students have already scrolled past.

    Grades 3–5~35 minCampaign Signals · The Coin Clapper Election

    Read the plan→
