The Mayor's Office

What a mayor actually does, and why two cities the same size can be run completely differently. Compares the strong-mayor and city-manager systems.

Grades 3–5~35 minNonpartisan

Word document — open it in Word, Pages, or Google Docs and adapt it for your class.

Students will learn what a mayor does, understand that different cities are organized in different ways, and compare the Strong Mayor and City Manager systems through hands-on activities and teamwork.

01 Warm-up 5 min

Warm-Up Question: "Imagine our classroom is a city. If something was broken, like the lights or the playground, who should fix it?"

02 Lecture 10 min

Opens w/quick slideshow presentation of around 10 minutes. (Note: going to use 2 examples of non-controversial or famous mayors, but will not point out specific party).

  • What is a City?
  • What Does a Mayor Do?
  • What is a Strong Mayor System?
  • What is a City Manager System?
  • Comparing the Two Systems
  • 2. Students are divided into groups and each group is assigned either a Strong Mayor system or a Council Manager system.
  • Each group is given 3–5 city problem cards (ex. a broken playground, a new park, or a community event).
  • Students discuss who would take the lead in solving each problem based on their city's form of government.
  • Groups share one of their solutions with the class.
  • 3. Students work in small groups.
  • Each group draws one community challenge from a hat (ex. build a new park, fix roads, improve the library, add a crosswalk, organize a town festival).
  • Students decide how their city's leaders would solve the challenge based on either a Strong Mayor System or a Council-Manager System.
  • Each group presents its solution to the class.
03 Wrap-up 5 min

4. Wrap up review and pass out candy.
